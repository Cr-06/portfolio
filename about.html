My Growth Story

1. The Starting Point (August – October)

I entered the CART Web App program with a very vague understanding of how the internet and coding worked, and every time I saw code it looked like magic to me. My first hurdle was after learning all the meaning of the code, staring at a blank VS Code file and being overwhelmed by the possibilities. Projects like BioShield taught me that while AI is a powerful tool, it is solely my job to understand the logic behind my code and the code that AI would generate.

2. The "Aha!" Moments (November – February)

The turning point came during the PawPal project. For the first time, HTML and CSS weren't separate in a full project in parts, rather unified code to build a website. I realized that coding isn't just about making things look good, but solving problems for users by how they interact with the page. I stopped working with my own ideas and expanded with my peers to figure out the best path for my team project.

3. The Professional Turn (March – May)

This portfolio represents my transition from a student who just "knows some code" to a developer who understands coding and all of the logic that goes into it. By implementing modern tools like CSS and JavaScript, I have proven that I can keep pace with a highly competitive field. I no longer just write lines of code, I build websites.

Detailed Project Experience

Programming: BioShield Website

With a classmate, I created a fully functional website with only HTML knowledge. With only HTML in our arsenal, we used our extensive AI literacy to help us along and create a project that could: take orders from users, by clicking "add to basket," items were added and the cost calculated from price and quantity selected by the user.

Programming: EECU Budget Calculator

EECU sponsored my school under the condition that students created a budget calculator for potential future use. For the majority of the project, only peers could work on it, requiring massive amounts of communication and coordination. I had to use my communication skills and pre-made goals so my peers had a clear vision to go off of.

Programming: PawPal

Working with one partner, we used our HTML and CSS knowledge to build a full multi-page website. The site showcases a pet tracker platform where users can follow their pet through a tracker attached by collar or other accessory.

Writing: Research Paper (How Habitat Design Shapes Animal Behavior)

After working with the Fresno Chaffee Zoo, our class wrote an essay built around what we learned on-site. I focused on how animals stay mentally engaged while living in contained habitats over most of their lives.

Writing: The Author to Her Book Presentation

In a team of three, we broke down the meaning of the poem "The Author to Her Book," created a slideshow, and presented our interpretation, including how the author connects the poem's language to her own personal experiences.
